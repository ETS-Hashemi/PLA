\section{Conclusion and future work}\label{sec:conclusion}

\pla{} turns a certainty-factor-style calculus into a verifiable
research vehicle: explicit semantics whose formulas execute as tests,
proved-and-corroborated convergence, saturation-free log-odds context
conditioning, convex weight learning tied directly to the engine, and
explanation fidelity measured against a control, all inside a
dependency-free package whose every reported number regenerates from
committed scripts.

The real-data studies show that (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}) eleven
readable rules sit $0.002$--$0.034$ per split below the interpretable
accuracy frontier on credit-card fraud across ten splits, that on the
credit-card study the learned model's log-loss beats the
constant-prevalence baseline, and that
rule-level traces beat reversed and permutation-averaged random
controls on every dataset. Alongside these sits a pre-registered
negative on
the context-conditioning mechanism itself: no practically useful
improvement on the pre-specified AUC endpoint under any of the three
designs, with the post-hoc credit-card average-precision gain held
apart as a hypothesis awaiting confirmation. The
negative scopes the next iteration precisely: confirming or refuting
that AP gain on new data, richer context features than single-bit
indicators, rule re-mining under vocabulary drift, and the full
serial-fraud protocol of~\cite{bao2020}. Beyond those, three
directions are specified with hypotheses and kill criteria in the
repository's gap statement: negation in the symbolic layer (making
constraint gating real), a human-subject study of trace utility in
audit decisions, and \pla{} as the uncertain-rule verifier in LLM
generate-and-verify pipelines~\cite{kambhampati2024}, a line whose
evaluation side symbolic provers have recently begun to
instrument~\cite{qi2025}.
