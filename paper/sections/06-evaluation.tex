\section{Experimental evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Status.} All numbers in this section are \emph{development
results} on a seeded synthetic sample with planted logistic ground truth
($n=2{,}000$; 28 Gaussian features, log-normal amounts, rare positives).
They validate the harness end to end; they do not establish real-world
performance. The pending study runs the identical scripts on the public
ULB credit-card fraud dataset~\cite{dalpozzolo2015} and, as a second
domain, the accounting-fraud data of~\cite{bao2020}.

\subsection{Setup}

Rows are split 70/30 with a fixed seed; quantile thresholds are fitted
on the training split only and map each feature to
\texttt{\_high}/\texttt{\_low} propositions, with a high-amount context
variable. Candidate rules (single and pair antecedents) are selected by
empirical precision with support floors; rule probabilities are the
empirical precisions --- measured, never invented. Metrics are ROC AUC,
Brier score, log-loss, and expected calibration error, implemented in
the package and unit-tested against hand-computed values.

\subsection{Head-to-head}

Table~\ref{tab:headtohead} compares \pla{} with static
empirical-precision rules (noisy-OR aggregation, neutral context),
\pla{} with learned weights (Section~\ref{sec:learning}), and four
baselines: logistic regression and gradient boosting on the raw numeric
features, the same \pla{} rules executed as a ProbLog program
(independent rules $=$ noisy-OR; deterministic test subsample), and a
pgmpy naive Bayes over the propositions.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Head-to-head on the synthetic development sample (30\% test);
generated by \texttt{scripts/run\_experiments.py} and reproduced
byte-for-byte from scratch.}
\label{tab:headtohead}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Model & AUC & Brier & log-loss & ECE\\
\midrule
logistic regression (raw features) & 0.9746 & 0.0413 & 0.1390 & 0.0298\\
gradient boosting (raw features) & 0.9171 & 0.0883 & 0.3750 & 0.0808\\
ProbLog on \pla{} rules (subsample) & 0.7416 & 0.1327 & 1.4447 & 0.0704\\
pgmpy naive Bayes (propositions) & 0.7228 & 0.0993 & 0.3405 & 0.0288\\
\pla{} static (empirical precisions) & 0.7217 & 0.1138 & 1.0908 & 0.0952\\
\pla{} learned (logit path) & 0.5945 & 0.1195 & 0.4780 & 0.1118\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three observations, reported as measured. First, the learned variant
wins decisively on calibration (log-loss $0.478$ vs.\ $1.091$; the
static rules' raw precisions are badly overconfident when several rules
fire together) but trails static on ranking (AUC $0.594$ vs.\ $0.722$).
We convergence-checked this: longer training improves the loss further
and does \emph{not} recover AUC, so it is not an under-training
artifact. The mechanism is visible in the setup: quantile discretization
discards most of the planted continuous signal, and maximum likelihood
spends what remains on calibration. Second, both \pla{} variants trail
the raw-feature baselines --- expected, since the synthetic ground truth
is logistic in exactly those features; the informative comparison on
real data is against equally interpretable baselines and \pla{}'s own
static ablation. Third, the ProbLog row executes the same rules under
distribution semantics and lands near \pla{} static, cross-validating
the noisy-OR implementation across systems.

\subsection{Trace fidelity}

Explanation quality is measured, not asserted, with deletion metrics in
the faithfulness framing of~\cite{jacovi2020}: \emph{comprehensiveness}
(drop in prediction when the trace's top-ranked rule's facts are
removed) and \emph{sufficiency} (gap when only they are kept), against a
reversed-attribution control that ranks the same fired rules worst
first.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Trace fidelity on the synthetic development sample; generated
by \texttt{scripts/run\_fidelity.py} and reproduced byte-for-byte.}
\label{tab:fidelity}
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
Model & Attribution & $n$ & Comprehensiveness & Sufficiency\\
\midrule
\pla{} static & trace & 350 & 0.2222 & 0.0527\\
\pla{} static & reversed control & 350 & 0.1864 & 0.1016\\
\pla{} learned & trace & 350 & 0.1425 & $-0.0656$\\
\pla{} learned & reversed control & 350 & 0.1182 & $-0.0421$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For both variants the faithful trace out-scores the control on
comprehensiveness (Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}): the traces name factors
that actually drive predictions. Hand-computed unit tests pin the metric
definitions themselves.

\subsection{The pending real-data study}

The scripts accept the real dataset directly
(\texttt{run\_experiments.py --data creditcard.csv}) once fetched by the
repository's loader, which verifies the download against the dataset's
published invariants (284{,}807 rows, 492 frauds, 31 columns). The
pre-registered question, with its kill criterion recorded in the
repository's gap statement, is whether context-conditioned learned
weights beat their own static ablation on temporally drifting splits;
a negative answer will be reported as such.
