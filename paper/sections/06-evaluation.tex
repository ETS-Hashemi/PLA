\section{Experimental evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets and splits} Four designs, all regenerated by the
committed scripts. (i)~A seeded synthetic harness validation
($n{=}2{,}000$, planted logistic ground truth; 70/30 split).
(ii)~The ULB credit-card fraud data~\cite{dalpozzolo2015}
(284{,}807 transactions, 492 frauds, 0.17\%; verified against its
published invariants) under ten random 70/30 splits (seeds 42--51;
199{,}364 train / 85{,}443 test; seeds beyond 44 skip only the
ProbLog/pgmpy cross-check rows). (iii)~The official replication data of
Bao et al.~\cite{bao2020} (146{,}045 firm-years, 964 AAER frauds) under
a \emph{strict} temporal design (train through fiscal 2001, gap 2002,
test from 2003) and (iv)~a \emph{SOX-boundary} design (train through
2005 --- spanning the 2003 regime change, so a PostSOX context weight is
identifiable --- gap 2006--07, test from 2008). Bao training sets drop
post-first-fraud firm-years, approximating the original serial-fraud
protocol (602 and 1{,}172 firm-years dropped respectively; counts
recorded in the generated files); firm-years with unparseable ratios are
complete-case deleted for every model alike.

\paragraph{Propositionalization and rule mining} Continuous features
become propositions via decile thresholds ($q{=}0.1$ and $q{=}0.9$,
fitted on the training split only): \texttt{V14\_low},
\texttt{Amount\_high}, and so on. Candidate rules are mined on the
training split by empirical precision with support floors: the top 8
single-proposition rules (support $\ge 20$) plus the top 3 pair rules
(support $\ge 10$, pairs drawn from the top 5 singles) --- 11 rules per
design, each carrying its empirical precision as the static confidence.
No per-dataset tuning of these mining constants was performed; they are
the pipeline defaults.

\paragraph{\pla{} variants} \texttt{static} scores by noisy-OR over
the fired rules' empirical precisions (the engine's default semantics;
the ProbLog row cross-checks the fold). \texttt{learned} fits rule and
context weights plus the intercept through the logit path
(Section~\ref{sec:learning}; full-batch gradient descent, 400 epochs,
learning rate $1.0$). \texttt{learned no-context} is the pre-registered
ablation: identical training with context variables removed, isolating
the context mechanism.

\paragraph{Baselines} Two interpretable competitors of comparable but
not identical complexity --- a depth-4 class-balanced decision tree
(at most 15 internal splits, against \pla{}'s 11 rules over the same
propositions) and an Explainable Boosting Machine at library defaults
(one shape function per continuous feature: a larger
model)~\cite{lou2013,nori2019} --- plus logistic
regression (raw features, \texttt{max\_iter}=2000), gradient boosting
(scikit-learn histogram GBT) both untuned and class-weighted, ProbLog
executing \pla{}'s own rules under distribution semantics (per-example
programs; deterministic subset, $n{=}150$, for compilation cost), and a
pgmpy naive Bayes over the same propositions (deterministic subset,
$n{=}2000$). Subset rows are reported for cross-validation of the
semantics, not for ranking comparison.

\paragraph{Metrics and inference} ROC AUC and average precision (AP;
the class-imbalance-sensitive complement) both carry seeded
percentile-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals ($B{=}500$, shared
resamples), and the tables display both. Claims about
\emph{differences} between models never rest on overlapping individual
intervals: every such claim cites a paired-bootstrap CI of the
difference, computed on shared resamples of the shared test set and
recorded in the generated \texttt{\_diffs} files. Calibration is
measured by log-loss against an explicit \emph{null baseline} --- a
constant predictor at the training prevalence, which appears as a row
in every table; it is a reference point, not a floor, since better
predictors score below it --- together with expected calibration
error over 10 equal-width bins, a
coarse instrument at these prevalences (nearly all mass falls in the
first bin), so the log-binned reliability diagram
(Figure~\ref{fig:reliability}) is the primary calibration evidence.
All values are exactly as generated (four decimals); prevalence sets
the AP baseline of a random ranker (e.g.\ $0.0017$ on credit card).

Four findings. First, the accuracy frontier on this dataset is itself
interpretable: EBM tops the seed-42 table on both metrics (AUC
$0.9815$, AP $0.8642$), just above logistic regression, and leads
eight of the ten splits (logistic regression and class-weighted
gradient boosting take one each). Second, an 11-rule, fully hand-traceable
\pla{} model tracks that frontier at a measured distance: across the
ten splits \pla{} static averages AUC $0.9660 \pm 0.0076$ (range
$0.9525$--$0.9812$) and learned $0.9602 \pm 0.0088$, against
$0.9741 \pm 0.0072$ for logistic regression and $0.9795 \pm 0.0056$
for EBM; the per-split gap to EBM spans $0.002$--$0.026$ for static
and $0.007$--$0.034$ for learned, with overlapping individual
intervals on every split. In average precision the static rules
retain most of the frontier's early ranking power
($0.6709 \pm 0.0382$ vs.\ $0.7564 \pm 0.0291$ for logistic
regression); the generated seed summary carries every per-split value.
Third, learning is a measured trade, not a free upgrade, and its
calibration gain must be stated against the right reference: the
learned variant's log-loss of $0.0113$ stands against $0.0127$ for the
constant-prevalence null baseline of Table~\ref{tab:creditcard} --- an
$11\%$ improvement over a no-skill predictor, while the raw-feature
frontier lands far below both --- and its ECE of $0.0022$ is read
knowing that equal-width bins are a blunt instrument here (the
null baseline scores ECE $0.0000$).
Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} shows
the learned model tracking the diagonal through the low-probability
bins where nearly all mass lives while the static rules' empirical
precisions overshoot --- but average precision reveals a real
early-ranking cost that AUC conceals, visible in the same figure as
score compression in the learned model's top bins: learned AP
averages $0.5534 \pm 0.0307$ across the ten splits against the static
rules' $0.6709 \pm 0.0382$, a paired-significant loss on nine of ten
splits (per-split intervals in the generated \texttt{\_diffs} files),
a loss concentrated exactly where an audit queue operates. Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
weighs this trade. Fourth, this study surfaced a model-class
pathology, preserved pre-fix in the repository's history: without the
intercept the learned variant's AUC was $0.076$ --- inverted --- for
the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:learning}; the intercept restores
it. A baseline note: untuned gradient boosting collapses at this class
imbalance (AUC $0.7159$), and simple class weighting repairs it (AUC
$0.9598$, AP $0.7899$) --- both rows are reported so neither defaults
nor a single tuning choice silently carries the comparison.

\paragraph{Scope of the interpretability claim} The ULB features are
anonymized principal components, so a proposition like
\texttt{V10\_low} is auditable arithmetic, not audit vocabulary: what
this dataset demonstrates is \emph{computational traceability} ---
every score decomposes into named rules with measured training-set
provenance --- not domain interpretability. The accounting study
carries the domain half: its propositions are financial-ratio
statements an auditor recognizes, and the top mined rules pair a large
receivables change with divergent cash sales or free cash flow
(\texttt{dch\_rec\_high} $\wedge$ \texttt{ch\_cs\_high},
\texttt{dch\_rec\_high} $\wedge$ \texttt{ch\_fcf\_low}) --- textbook
accrual-manipulation red flags --- though there the discrimination
under drift is weak. Neither study alone covers the full
interpretability claim; jointly they scope it, and
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} records the boundary.

\subsection{Real data II: accounting fraud under temporal drift}

Two temporal designs on the Bao et al.\ data (13 discretized ratios;
issuance indicator as context; the SOX design adds a PostSOX context
bit). In the strict design (62{,}087 training firm-years after
serial-fraud handling; 58{,}405 test), tail rules mined pre-2002 barely
rank later frauds: \pla{} static $0.5285$ [0.5028, 0.5585], learned $0.4905$,
no-context ablation $0.4733$, against $0.6794$ for raw-ratio logistic
regression and $0.6660$ for EBM. Average precision shows how thin the
absolute signal is even for the winners: logistic regression reaches
$0.0112$ against a test prevalence of $0.0061$ --- less than double the
random-ranker baseline. The context ablation shows a small positive AUC
gap ($+0.0172$) that the paired interval confirms is real
([$0.0019$, $0.0373$]) --- weighed with the verdict below, since both
models rank below chance here. The SOX-boundary
design (82{,}630 training firm-years after serial-fraud handling;
32{,}530 test; Table~\ref{tab:sox}, Figure~\ref{fig:sox}) was built to
give the context weight its best chance.

\paragraph{The pre-registered verdict on context conditioning} The
claim rests on a paired-bootstrap interval on the \emph{difference},
not on overlapping individual intervals: under the design built to
make the context weight identifiable, learned minus ablation is
$\Delta\text{AUC} = 0.0011$ [$-0.0071$, $0.0140$] and
$\Delta\text{AP} = 0.0001$ [$-0.0000$, $0.0009$], bounding the effect
to $|\Delta\text{AUC}| \le 0.014$ at these sample sizes. We state this
as a \emph{bound}, not a formal equivalence: the pre-registration
(repository gap statement, commit \texttt{8f2dd2e}) specified only the
directional criterion --- the context model must beat its own ablation
--- and named no practical-equivalence margin, so no equivalence test
is licensed; readers with a margin in mind can apply it to the
interval. On credit card the paired ablation $\Delta$AUC contains zero
on all ten splits. The strict design shows what the paired instrument
can detect: a small real effect,
$\Delta\text{AUC} = +0.0172$ [$0.0019$, $0.0373$] --- between two
models that both rank \emph{below chance} ($0.4905$ vs.\ $0.4733$,
against static $0.5285$ and logistic regression $0.6794$). The
defensible conclusion, stated with its scope: \emph{context
conditioning produced no practically useful improvement on the
pre-specified AUC endpoint under any of the three designs; a
consistent post-hoc average-precision improvement on the credit-card
data --- paired interval excluding zero on all ten splits,
$\Delta$AP $+0.038$ to $+0.096$, and absent under both drift designs
--- requires confirmation on new data before it can count as a
benefit.} We flag the tension openly: AP is the metric this paper
elsewhere ties to audit queues, which is exactly why the credit-card
AP result is a hypothesis worth confirming rather than a footnote ---
and exactly why, being post hoc, it cannot revise a verdict defined on
the pre-registered endpoint. The demonstrated value of learning lies
in calibration, not in context adjustment;
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} draws the drift lesson.

\subsection{Trace fidelity}

\paragraph{What is being tested, and what would be circular} The trace
is generated from the model's own rule computation, so no deletion
metric here can certify external explanation quality; what it can
certify --- and what a bug would break --- is that the \emph{exported
ranking} matches what actually moves the score. For the learned model
that match is exact by construction on the log-odds scale: the
telescoped path of Section~\ref{sec:learning} makes rule $r$'s
contribution identically $z_r$, so its rule-level log-odds
comprehensiveness \emph{equals} the trace's top score (a unit test
asserts the identity). The genuinely falsifiable measurements are
therefore (i) the static model's, whose precision ranking is a
heuristic that could have failed, and (ii) the margins over two
controls that re-rank the \emph{same fired rules}: reversed
(worst-first) and a deterministic random ordering. We report
\emph{rule-level} deletion --- the top-ranked rule is removed from the
fold with the facts untouched --- because fact deletion can deactivate
several overlapping rules at once (deleting one antecedent of a pair
rule also silences the matching single rules), which inflates
fact-level scores; both levels are in the generated files.

Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} reports the static model. Its precision
ranking beats both controls on every dataset, with paired-bootstrap
margins whose intervals exclude zero throughout. The rule-level
numbers also quantify the confound the design removes: fact-level
comprehensiveness on credit card is $0.0202$, inflated about seven
percent above the rule-level $0.0188$ by deletions that silence
overlapping rules (synthetic: $0.2222$ vs.\ $0.2006$). For the learned
model, the rule-level log-odds trace equals $z_{\text{top}}$
identically (the unit-tested exactness above), and its
probability-scale deltas compress to the display floor on rare-event
data ($0.0002$ at $n{=}48{,}630$) --- consistent with calibration,
uninformative as a measurement, which is why the log-odds scale is the
right instrument for it. Every level, scale, and control combination
is in the generated files.

\subsection{Case study: one real fraud, traced}\label{sec:casestudy}

The generated exhibit (\texttt{results/case\_study\_creditcard.md})
walks the highest-scored true positive in the credit-card test split
through the engine. Eleven rules fire; the noisy-OR fold accumulates
line by line from the strongest pair rule
(V10\_low $\wedge$ V4\_high, empirical precision $0.1949$) to a static
score of $0.4929$ against a base rate of $0.0017$. The deletion
counterfactual quantifies the top factor: removing its two facts drops
the static score from $0.4929$ to $0.2552$ (learned: $0.0189$ to
$0.0069$), and a leave-one-rule-out table gives every fired rule's
marginal contribution with the facts untouched --- for the learned
model its log-odds column equals $z_r$ identically, the exact
attribution. This artifact --- weighted rules whose provenance is
an auditable training-set measurement, folded by explicit arithmetic,
with a counterfactual attached --- is what none of the baselines
produce, and is the deliverable the audit setting requires.
