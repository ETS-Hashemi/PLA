\begin{abstract}
We present \pla{}, a rule-confidence model for domains where inherent
interpretability is a requirement rather than a preference. \pla{}
descends openly from MYCIN-style certainty factors: rules carry
confidences, conjunction is the G\"odel t-norm, and co-supporting rules
combine through a named, per-knowledge-base aggregation operator
(noisy-OR by default --- provably MYCIN's parallel-combination rule).
Three properties distinguish it from its lineage and from modern
statistical-relational systems: (i)~rule confidences are
\emph{context-conditioned}, with a log-odds mode in which context
variables contribute additive evidence weights that never saturate;
(ii)~both rule and context weights are \emph{learnable} --- on binary
facts the engine's log-odds path reduces exactly to logistic regression
over rule firings, making the loss convex and training provably
monotone; and (iii)~explanations are \emph{traces whose fidelity is
measured}, via deletion-based comprehensiveness and sufficiency against
a reversed-attribution control. The calculus has machine-checked
convergence guarantees, the entire engine is a few hundred lines of
dependency-free Python, and every experimental number regenerates
byte-for-byte from committed scripts. Development results on synthetic
data validate the harness; the real-data evaluation on public fraud
datasets is the pending experiment that will decide whether context
conditioning earns its keep.
\end{abstract}
