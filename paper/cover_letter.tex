Dear Editors of \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems},

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, \emph{``PLA: A Transparent,
Learnable Rule-Confidence Engine with Measured Trace Fidelity''}, for
publication as a full-length article.

The paper presents a rule-confidence engine for settings --- audit,
compliance, education --- where inherent interpretability is a
requirement rather than a preference. Its contributions are: an
explicit certainty-factor-style calculus whose formal definitions are
executable and whose fixpoint convergence is machine-checked; a convex
reduction of rule- and context-weight learning to logistic regression
over rule firings, tied exactly to the engine that executes the fitted
model; and explanation traces whose fidelity is \emph{measured} against
a reversed-attribution control rather than asserted. On real
credit-card fraud data, an 11-rule, fully hand-traceable model comes
within 0.01--0.03 AUC of the interpretable accuracy frontier with
near-perfect calibration; on accounting-fraud data under two temporal
designs, pre-registered ablations find no reliable ranking benefit from
the context-conditioning mechanism, and the paper reports that negative
result as such, with the design lesson it carries for drift adaptation.

Every number in the manuscript regenerates from committed, seeded
scripts with bootstrap confidence intervals; claims about competitor
systems are enforced as tests against the installed packages; and a
claim-provenance appendix maps each substantive statement to its
verifying artifact. The complete source is available to reviewers at
\url{https://github.com/ETS-Hashemi/PLA}.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and
has no preprint or prior version. The author declares no competing
interests.

Thank you for your consideration.
